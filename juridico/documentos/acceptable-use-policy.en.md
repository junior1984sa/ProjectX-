## 0. Why this Policy exists — read this first

ProspectX sends email through **shared sending infrastructure**. Your domain, your reputation and your deliverability sit alongside every other Subscriber's.

That has a hard consequence: **one Subscriber who spams can degrade or destroy the Service for everyone else.** Blocklist operators and mailbox providers do not act against a single account — they act against a sending domain, an IP range or a provider relationship. Our own email supplier can terminate us for the conduct of one of our customers.

So this Policy is **not a formality and not a statement of aspiration**. It is infrastructure protection. We enforce it mechanically where we can and by suspension where we cannot, and Section 12 explains that we will sometimes act **first** and explain afterwards. That is a deliberate choice, and you are agreeing to it.

A second consequence follows: **some of the restrictions here are stricter than the law.** Where the law is unsettled, or where being wrong would cost us the platform, we choose the conservative rule. Being permitted by law is not the same as being permitted here.

## 2. The single rule everything else follows from

**Before you contact anyone, you must have a lawful basis to contact that specific recipient, in that recipient's jurisdiction, on that channel.**

2.1 **You decide who is contacted; you are the sender.** We supply search, drafting and delivery. We do not know your offer, your history with a recipient, or whether a prior relationship exists. Only you do.

2.2 **A record appearing in our search results is not evidence of consent, and we never present it as such.** Business Records are derived from OpenStreetMap, a public database edited by volunteers. **A record may have been created by someone other than the business itself.** It therefore cannot support an argument that the business published its address inviting contact, and it cannot support inferred consent.

2.3 **The absence of a technical block is not legal permission.** Our country restrictions (Section 5) are a crude, conservative filter. They do not assess your specific offer, your specific recipient or your specific relationship. Passing the filter means we did not stop you; it does not mean the message is lawful.

2.4 **The channel does not change the rule.** Several jurisdictions treat instant messaging — including WhatsApp — the same way they treat email. In the United Kingdom the applicable rules on electronic marketing extend to instant messaging, and in Australia the Spam Act 2003 (Cth) covers commercial electronic messages generally (jurisdiction: UK and Australia; sources: ico.org.uk and legislation.gov.au, consulted 15 August 2026). **Sending manually, one message at a time, from your own phone, is not a defence** — the rules look at the nature of the message, not at the degree of automation. If you may not email a recipient, assume you may not message them either, and take your own advice before doing so.

## 7. The suppression list

7.1 Every unsubscribe is recorded on a **global suppression list that applies to all Subscribers**, not only to the one who sent the message. If a recipient opts out of your message, no ProspectX Subscriber can contact that address through the Service again.

7.2 This is deliberate, and it is not negotiable. It is the single control that keeps one Subscriber's rejected contact from being re-contacted by the next five.

7.3 **The list is never readable by any Subscriber.** The platform answers only "may this address be contacted — yes or no". You cannot download it, query it in bulk, or use it as a data source.

7.4 You must not attempt to circumvent suppression, including by exporting a suppressed record and contacting it outside the Service, by re-uploading it, or by contacting the same business at a different address after an opt-out.

7.5 Suppression is not the same as erasure. A suppression entry is kept indefinitely, precisely so that the request keeps working (Privacy Policy, Part 6).
