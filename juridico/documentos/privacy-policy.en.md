### 1.2 Is this personal data at all?

Often it is. A business address such as `contato@nomedapessoa.com`, or the contact details of a sole trader, identifies an individual. **We treat all business contact records as personal data by default**, rather than assuming they are not.

### 1.3 Where the data comes from — attribution

Business location data is derived from **OpenStreetMap**, © **OpenStreetMap contributors**, under the **Open Database License (ODbL) v1.0**. OpenStreetMap is a public, collaboratively edited database. **Records are created and edited by volunteers. Neither we nor the OpenStreetMap Foundation verify them, and neither is a source of consent to be contacted.**

### 1.5 The four conditions for outbound email

We only permit a first commercial approach through the Service where **all four** are true:
1. the address is a **business** address, not a personal one;
2. the message is **relevant to the recipient's stated line of business**;
3. the sender is **clearly and truthfully identified**, including a physical postal address;
4. a **working one-click unsubscribe** is present in every message.

Conditions 3 and 4 are enforced by the platform: the footer is added server-side and cannot be removed by the subscriber.

### 1.6 Where we do not rely on legitimate interests

**United Kingdom.** The Privacy and Electronic Communications (EC Directive) Regulations 2003 ("PECR"), regulation 22, distinguish "corporate subscribers" from "individual subscribers". The ICO's published position is that **sole traders and ordinary partnerships are individual subscribers**, and unsolicited electronic marketing to them requires prior consent or a soft opt-in based on a previous sale. **A legitimate-interests analysis does not substitute for that consent.** PECR also treats instant messaging as "electronic mail", so the same rule applies to WhatsApp; sending manually, one at a time, is not a defence.

**Australia.** The Spam Act 2003 (Cth) requires consent — express or inferred — for commercial electronic messages. **Consent cannot be inferred from an OpenStreetMap record**, because the record may have been created by a third party and does not represent a publication by the business itself.

Accordingly, the Service applies destination restrictions described in the Acceptable Use Policy, and the subscriber must confirm a lawful basis before sending. `[PREENCHER: confirm the current state of the destination blocks in the product before publishing — see PARECER]`

### 1.7 Why we do not notify each business individually

Under UK GDPR article 14(5)(b), the duty to inform an individual whose data was obtained indirectly does not apply where doing so would involve disproportionate effort, provided appropriate measures are taken — including making the information publicly available. **This policy is that measure.** In addition, every message sent through the Service tells the recipient why they received it and how to stop it, which discharges the substance of the duty at the moment of contact. The equivalent reasoning applies to LGPD article 9.

### 1.8 We do not sell this data

We do not sell, rent or license business contact records as a standalone data product. Subscribers pay for access to the Service. `[PREENCHER: if the product ever adds bulk export or a data feed as a separate SKU, this sentence must be revisited — a "sale" under some US state laws is broader than payment for a list]`
